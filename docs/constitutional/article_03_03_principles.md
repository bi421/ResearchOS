# ResearchOS — Constitution

## Article III: Principles

### 3.1 Research Principles

#### 3.1.1 The Scientific Method

All research within ResearchOS follows the scientific method:

1. **Observation:** Identify market phenomena worthy of investigation.
2. **Question:** Formulate a specific, testable research question.
3. **Hypothesis:** Propose a falsifiable explanation or prediction.
4. **Prediction:** Derive specific, measurable predictions from the hypothesis.
5. **Test:** Compare predictions against real market data.
6. **Analysis:** Determine whether the hypothesis is supported or refuted.
7. **Conclusion:** Update knowledge state and identify new questions.

#### 3.1.2 Falsifiability

Every research output must be falsifiable. A statement that cannot be proven wrong is not research. If a scenario does not specify what market conditions would invalidate it, the scenario is incomplete.

#### 3.1.3 Reproducibility

All research must be reproducible. Given the same data and the same methodology, the same conclusions must follow. Randomness must be explicitly controlled and documented.

#### 3.1.4 Transparency

Every conclusion must be traceable to its supporting data and reasoning. Black-box analysis is not permitted. If a conclusion cannot be explained, it cannot be trusted.

#### 3.1.5 Multi-Factor Analysis

No single indicator or model determines a research conclusion. All significant conclusions must integrate evidence from at least two independent analytical dimensions.

#### 3.1.6 Conservatism in Uncertainty

When uncertainty is high, the system must explicitly state the range of uncertainty rather than producing false precision. A probability estimate without a confidence interval is incomplete.

### 3.2 Human-AI Collaboration Principles

#### 3.2.1 Human Primacy

The human trader is always the principal. AI systems are assistants. No AI output can override, bypass, or diminish human authority.

#### 3.2.2 Augmentation, Not Automation

AI augments human capability; it does not replace human judgment. The goal is a more capable human, not a human-free system.

#### 3.2.3 Transparency of AI Contribution

All AI-generated research must be clearly labeled. The human must always know which parts of the research were machine-generated and which were human-originated.

#### 3.2.4 Right to Explanation

The human has the right to request and receive a plain-language explanation of how any research conclusion was reached.

#### 3.2.5 Right to Disagree

The human may reject any research output without justification. The system must respect this decision and may not penalize or override it.

### 3.3 Data Principles

#### 3.3.1 Data Integrity

All data used in research must be verified for accuracy, completeness, and timeliness. Corrupted or stale data must be clearly flagged.

#### 3.3.2 Source Attribution

Every piece of data must be traceable to its original source. Synthetic or derived data must be explicitly marked as such.

#### 3.3.3 Look-Ahead Bias Prevention

Any analysis that uses data not available at the time of the research is invalid. Strict temporal ordering must be enforced.

#### 3.3.4 Data Minimization

Only data relevant to the research question should be used. Collecting data without a clear analytical purpose is prohibited.
